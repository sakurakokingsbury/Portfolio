Featured Project

Interactive Robotic Pet

Designed and prototyped an autonomous robotic pet capable of movement, emotional expression, and interactive user responses through integrated sensors, embedded electronics, and a fully custom 3D-printed chassis. The project was awarded 2nd place among our class of ME322 engineering design projects.

Project Overview

Designed and prototyped an autonomous robotic pet capable of movement, emotional expression, and user interaction through integrated sensors, embedded electronics, and Arduino-based behavior logic.

The system used ultrasonic sensing, push buttons, a photoresistor, RGB LEDs, an LCD display, and DC motors to simulate emotional states including happiness, loneliness, sadness, and hunger while autonomously roaming and responding to user input.

The project combined mechanical design, electronics integration, embedded programming, and rapid prototyping into a compact 3D-printed consumer-style robotic platform.

Mechanical Design

The chassis was designed in SolidWorks as a compact electromechanical platform capable of housing the drivetrain, sensors, battery, microcontroller, and wiring within a durable PLA shell.

The final assembly consisted of 5 printed body sections including the chassis, face cage, 2 rear panels, and an articulated tail. Mounts were designed directly into the chassis to secure the microcontroller, breadboard, LCD display, motors, ultrasonic sensor, buttons, and photoresistor without requiring complex secondary brackets.

Multiple iterations refined component spacing, structural stability, wire routing, and drivetrain performance. Reinforced button supports and enclosed electronics improved durability during repeated testing and user interaction.

The final design balanced compact packaging, manufacturability, structural durability, and approachable product aesthetics within a fully integrated robotic assembly.

Design Iteration

A major focus of this project was iterative mechanical refinement. The robotic pet underwent multiple redesigns throughout development to improve structural durability, electronics integration, manufacturability, drivetrain performance, and overall system reliability. Each iteration addressed issues discovered during physical testing and full system integration.

Initial Prototype

Early iterations focused on establishing the overall body shape, chassis dimensions, and initial component layout. These versions helped visualize the product concept, but lacked the mounting geometry and internal packaging required for a functional system.

Internal Packaging Redesign

The chassis was redesigned around the physical dimensions of the electronics, including the Arduino, breadboard, battery, LCD display, motors, and sensors. Additional integrated supports and retention geometry were added to improve component stability and organization.

Structural and Mobility Improvements

Later revisions reinforced the body shell, improved motor mounting geometry, and redesigned the rear wheel system to improve turning performance and reduce resistance during autonomous movement.

Sensor and Interaction Integration

Additional iterations focused on integrating the ultrasonic sensor, LCD screen, RGB LED, push buttons, and photoresistor into the outer shell while maintaining a clean appearance and preserving user accessibility.

Final Assembly Refinement

The final iteration introduced enclosed electronics packaging, reinforced button supports, improved structural alignment between body panels, integrated mounting geometry, and a more refined external appearance suitable for repeated testing and interaction.

Electronics and System Architecture

  • Arduino microcontroller for autonomous behavior and emotional state control
  • Ultrasonic sensor for obstacle detection and autonomous movement
  • LCD display for facial expressions and emotional feedback
  • RGB LED for additional emotional state indication
  • Push buttons and photoresistor for interactive user input
  • DC motors and drivetrain system for autonomous roaming behavior

The electronics architecture prioritized compact packaging, protected wiring, and accessible component placement within the limited chassis volume.

The system was first mapped and simulated in Tinkercad before physical integration. Wiring layout, component placement, and power routing were refined throughout development to improve reliability and simplify troubleshooting during testing.

Autonomous Behavior Logic

The software architecture simulated an evolving emotional personality using randomized state generation, timed behavior loops, environmental sensing, and user interaction.

Push buttons and the photoresistor simulated feeding, petting, and attention, allowing users to directly influence the pet’s emotional state. Different emotional conditions controlled movement behavior, LCD facial expressions, RGB LED outputs, and autonomous roaming patterns.

A dynamic happiness scale was also integrated into the behavior logic. As the user interacted positively with the pet, its happiness value increased and unlocked additional behaviors and “tricks,” including more energetic movement patterns and playful responses. Lower emotional states instead produced slower movement and more passive behavior.

Ultrasonic sensing enabled obstacle detection and autonomous navigation while emotional state logic continuously modified movement speed, interaction behavior, and emotional outputs throughout operation.

Challenges and Engineering Decisions

Initial CAD Packaging Constraints

Early chassis concepts did not properly account for the real dimensions of the electronic components or the required mounting geometry. The design was completely rebuilt around the physical hardware to improve packaging, structural stability, and manufacturability.

Button and Shell Durability

Initial button mounting geometry allowed excessive movement during repeated interaction, creating risk of structural damage to the shell. Reinforced internal support brackets and tighter mounting tolerances were added to improve long-term durability.

Rear Wheel Movement Resistance

The original rear axle configuration created excessive resistance during turning and reduced the effectiveness of autonomous movement. The rear support system was redesigned using a ball-and-socket wheel configuration to improve maneuverability and drivetrain consistency.

Electronics Integration and Wire Routing

Integrating multiple electronic components into a compact chassis created challenges involving wire routing, accessibility, and component interference. Multiple layout revisions were required to organize the internal system while maintaining reliable operation.

Balancing Emotional Logic and Autonomous Behavior

One of the largest software challenges was balancing the interaction between emotional state transitions, user input, and autonomous movement behavior. Extensive testing and tuning were required to create stable behavior loops that felt responsive while maintaining consistent operation.

Limited Hardware Constraints

The provided electronics kit imposed limitations on available motors, displays, and component options. Several system decisions, including changing the LCD configuration and refining drivetrain behavior, were made to maximize functionality within the available hardware.

Future Improvements

Although the final prototype successfully demonstrated autonomous interaction, emotional simulation, and integrated movement behavior, several future improvements could further increase realism, reliability, movement capability, and overall user experience.

Improved Drivetrain Performance

Future iterations could incorporate stronger motors and improved wheel traction to increase movement speed, improve maneuverability, and allow more reliable operation across different floor surfaces.

Expanded Sensor Coverage

Additional ultrasonic sensors and photoresistors would improve environmental awareness, obstacle avoidance accuracy, and responsiveness during autonomous movement and user interaction.

Expanded Emotional Expression

Future versions of the pet could integrate an actuated tail using a servo motor to create physical tail-wagging behavior during happy emotional states. Additional audio feedback could also be implemented using an onboard speaker capable of producing barking, whimpering, and other animal-like sounds tied directly to the robot’s emotional state logic.

Voice Interaction and Smart Behaviors

A microphone system could be integrated to allow for voice commands, verbal interaction, and simple trick-based behaviors. Future software improvements could also expand the emotional logic system with more advanced autonomous behaviors, memory-based interaction, and dynamic personality simulation.

Final Engineering Takeaways

This project provided extensive experience in electromechanical product design, embedded system integration, rapid prototyping, and cross-disciplinary engineering problem solving. Developing a fully interactive robotic system required balancing mechanical design, electronics packaging, autonomous behavior, manufacturability, and user interaction within a compact platform.

The project also reinforced the importance of iterative testing and the close relationship between mechanical and electrical design. Small physical changes to component placement, mounting geometry, wheel configuration, wiring layout, and structural supports had direct impacts on system reliability and autonomous behavior.
