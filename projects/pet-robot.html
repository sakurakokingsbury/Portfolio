Featured Project

Interactive Robotic Toy Pet

Designed and prototyped an autonomous robotic toy pet capable of movement, emotional expression, and interactive user responses through integrated sensors, embedded electronics, and a fully custom 3D-printed chassis. The project was awarded 2nd place among our class of ME322 engineering design projects.

Project Overview

This project focused on the design, manufacturing, and integration of an autonomous robotic toy pet intended to simulate companionship through movement, emotional expression, and user interaction. The system combined custom mechanical design, embedded electronics, sensor integration, and Arduino-based behavior logic into a compact consumer-style prototype.

The toy pet continuously interpreted user inputs and environmental conditions through push buttons, a photoresistor, and ultrasonic sensing to transition between emotional states such as happiness, sadness, loneliness, and hunger. A major focus of the project was balancing expressive behavior, compact packaging, manufacturability, electronics integration, structural durability, and autonomous movement.

Throughout development, the design underwent multiple mechanical and electrical iterations to improve component packaging, button supports, sensor integration, drivetrain stability, and overall system reliability. The final prototype successfully integrated autonomous movement, emotional state simulation, LCD facial expressions, obstacle detection, and interactive user feedback into a fully functional robotic pet.

Mechanical Design

The toy pet chassis was designed in SolidWorks as a compact integrated platform capable of housing the mechanical, electrical, and sensing systems required for autonomous interaction and movement. A major design goal was create a lovable companion while maintaining structural durability, organized and safe component packaging, accessible electronics, and a randomized dynamic persoanlity.

The final assembly consisted of multiple interconnected PLA body sections, including the chassis, face assembly, articulated tail, rear panels, and wheel support components. Custom mounting geometry was integrated directly into the chassis to securely house the Arduino, breadboard, DC motors, battery, ultrasonic sensor, LCD display, buttons, and photoresistor while minimizing the need for a complex assembly.

Multiple design iterations were required to balance component spacing, structural integrity, manufacturability, wire routing, and consistently reliable operation. Internal supports and reinforced button brackets were added directly onto the main sections to improve durability during repeated testing including rough petting, shoving, compressing, and dropping.

The final design incorporated improved electronics packaging, reinforced mounts, enclosed wiring, integrated sensor openings, and improved drivetrain stability while maintaining a compact and aesthetically cohesive robotic pet platform.

Design Iteration

A major focus of this project was iterative mechanical refinement. The toy pet underwent multiple redesigns throughout development to improve structural durability, electronics integration, manufacturability, drivetrain performance, and overall system reliability. Each iteration addressed issues discovered during physical testing and full system integration.

Initial Prototype

Early iterations focused on establishing the overall body shape, chassis dimensions, and initial component layout. These versions helped visualize the product concept, but lacked the mounting geometry and internal packaging required for a functional system.

Internal Packaging Redesign

The chassis was redesigned around the physical dimensions of the electronics, including the Arduino, breadboard, battery, LCD display, motors, and sensors. Additional integrated supports and retention geometry were added to improve component stability and organization.

Structural and Mobility Improvements

Later revisions reinforced the body shell, improved motor mounting geometry, and redesigned the rear wheel system to improve turning performance and reduce resistance during autonomous movement.

Sensor and Interaction Integration

Additional iterations focused on integrating the ultrasonic sensor, LCD screen, RGB LED, push buttons, and photoresistor into the outer shell while maintaining a clean appearance and preserving user accessibility.

Final Assembly Refinement

The final iteration introduced enclosed electronics packaging, reinforced button supports, improved structural alignment between body panels, integrated mounting geometry, and a more refined external appearance suitable for repeated testing and interaction.

Electronics and System Architecture

  • Arduino microcontroller for autonomous behavior and emotional state control
  • Ultrasonic sensor for obstacle detection and autonomous movement
  • LCD display for facial expressions and emotional feedback
  • RGB LED for additional emotional state indication
  • Push buttons and photoresistor for interactive user input
  • DC motors and drivetrain system for autonomous roaming behavior

The system architecture was designed to balance compact packaging, maintainability, electrical reliability, and structural protection within the limited body volume of the toy pet. Component placement was carefully arranged to reduce wire interference, improve accessibility, and maintain a clean overall system layout.

The electronics system was initially mapped and simulated in Tinkercad before being physically integrated into the prototype. Wiring organization, component placement, and system packaging were refined throughout development to improve reliability and simplify troubleshooting during repeated testing.

Autonomous Behavior Logic

The toy pet’s software architecture was designed to simulate an evolving emotional personality through randomized emotional states, user interaction, timed behavior loops, and environmental sensing. Using programmed state logic, the system continuously transitioned between emotional conditions such as happiness, sadness, loneliness, and hunger.

User interaction played a major role in modifying the pet’s emotional state. Push buttons and the photoresistor were used to simulate actions such as feeding, petting, and attention, allowing the user to directly influence the toy pet’s mood and behavior. Emotional state changes triggered different outputs through the LCD display, RGB LED colors, sound behavior, and movement speed.

The autonomous roaming system used ultrasonic sensing to detect nearby obstacles and redirect movement during operation. Movement behavior was further influenced by the emotional state logic, allowing the robot to behave differently depending on whether it was happy, lonely, hungry, or sad.

Additional software refinement focused on improving emotional transitions, balancing interaction timing, stabilizing autonomous movement behavior, and creating smoother interaction between the sensor systems, emotional state loops, and physical outputs.

Challenges and Engineering Decisions

Initial CAD Packaging Constraints

Early chassis concepts did not properly account for the real dimensions of the electronic components or the required mounting geometry. The design was completely rebuilt around the physical hardware to improve packaging, structural stability, and manufacturability.

Button and Shell Durability

Initial button mounting geometry allowed excessive movement during repeated interaction, creating risk of structural damage to the shell. Reinforced internal support brackets and tighter mounting tolerances were added to improve long-term durability.

Rear Wheel Movement Resistance

The original rear axle configuration created excessive resistance during turning and reduced the effectiveness of autonomous movement. The rear support system was redesigned using a ball-and-socket wheel configuration to improve maneuverability and drivetrain consistency.

Electronics Integration and Wire Routing

Packaging a large number of electronic components into a compact chassis created challenges involving wire routing, accessibility, and component interference. Multiple layout revisions were required to organize the internal system while maintaining reliable operation.

Balancing Emotional Logic and Autonomous Behavior

One of the largest software challenges was balancing the interaction between emotional state transitions, user input, and autonomous movement behavior. Extensive testing and tuning were required to create stable behavior loops that felt responsive while maintaining consistent operation.

Limited Hardware Constraints

The provided electronics kit imposed limitations on available motors, displays, and component options. Several system decisions, including changing the LCD configuration and refining drivetrain behavior, were made to maximize functionality within the available hardware.

Future Improvements

Although the final prototype successfully demonstrated autonomous interaction, emotional simulation, and integrated movement behavior, several future improvements could further increase realism, reliability, movement capability, and overall user experience.

Improved Drivetrain Performance

Future iterations could incorporate stronger motors and improved wheel traction to increase movement speed, improve maneuverability, and allow more reliable operation across different floor surfaces.

Expanded Sensor Coverage

Additional sensors could improve environmental awareness, obstacle avoidance accuracy, and responsiveness during autonomous movement and user interaction.

More Advanced Emotional Behavior

Future software improvements could include more dynamic emotional transitions, expanded interaction behaviors, memory-based responses, and more advanced autonomous personality simulation.

Final Engineering Takeaways

This project provided extensive experience in electromechanical product design, embedded system integration, rapid prototyping, and cross-disciplinary engineering problem solving. Developing a fully interactive robotic system required balancing manufacturability, structural stability, electronics packaging, sensor integration, autonomous behavior, and user interaction within a compact platform.

The project also reinforced the importance of iterative testing and the close relationship between mechanical and electrical design. Small physical changes to component placement, mounting geometry, wheel configuration, wiring layout, and structural supports often had direct impacts on system reliability and autonomous behavior.

Beyond the technical work, the project strengthened collaborative engineering skills through rapid prototyping, debugging, hardware integration, and adapting to shifting project responsibilities throughout development.
