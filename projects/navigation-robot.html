Autonomous Navigation Robot

Designed and built a fully autonomous robot capable of navigating an obstacle course using ultrasonic sensing and coordinate-based pathfinding, completing the final course in 1 minute 21 seconds with zero collisions.

Robotics Mechanical Design Embedded Systems SolidWorks Arduino

Project Overview

This project focused on the design, manufacturing, and integration of a fully autonomous navigation robot capable of traversing a maze-like obstacle course. The system combined custom mechanical design via SolidWorks, embedded electronics, sensor integration, and autonomous navigation logic into a 3" x 5" mini rover.

The robot continuously interpreted real-time ultrasonic sensor data to detect walls and obstacles while navigating toward coordinate-based target locations. A major focus of the project was balancing reliable navigation performance with physical constraints such as chassis size, sensor placement, wire management, manufacturability, weight, speed, and overall system stability.

Throughout development, the design underwent multiple iterations to improve structural mounts, sensor coverage, component integration, and overall performance during testing and and the final run. The final robot successfully completed the course autonomously with zero collisions in 1 minute and 21 seconds.

Mechanical Design

The robot chassis was designed in SolidWorks as a compact, fully integrated platform capable of supporting the mechanical, electrical, and sensing systems required for autonomous navigation. A major design goal was minimizing overall size and weight while still maintaining stability, optimized sensor positioning, and easily accessible component placement.

The chassis was developed as a single 3D-printed structure to reduce assembly complexity, improve structural integrity, and simplify manufacturing. Stacked mounting slots were designed directly into the chassis to simplify ultrasonic sensors instalation, reduce screw count, and allow a third sensor to be added without compromising the size or complexity of the robot. Custom mounting features were also designed for the OLED display, electronics, motors, and supporting hardware, reducing the need for additional brackets.

Multiple design iterations were required to balance sensor performance, structural integrity, wiring, and consistantly reliable navigation. Sensor placement was especially important because small alignment changes significantly affected the accuracny of obstacle detection and wall tracking during autonomous navigation.

The final design incorporated reinforced mounting features, improved motor supports, organized wire management, and a crate-style electronics compartment that allowed the robot to remain compact while securely housing the electronics.

Although the final robot successfully completed the course autonomously with zero collisions, future design improvements could further increase performance and reliability. Additional integrated mounts within the crate-style electronics compartment would improve stability and help reduce vibration during autonomous movement. Future iterations could also further focus on reducing overall weight or incorporating gearing changes to improve driving speed.

Design Iteration

A major focus of this project was iterative mechanical refinement. The robot chassis underwent multiple redesigns throughout development to improve structural stability, sensor coverage, manufacturability, wiring organization, and overall autonomous performance. Each iteration addressed issues discovered during physical testing and system integration.

Initial Prototype

Early iterations focused on establishing the base chassis dimensions, mounting the drivetrain components, and creating initial sensor mounts for obstacle detection testing. The primary goal during this stage was verifying overall packaging and functionality.

Sensor System Redesign

The ultrasonic mounting system was redesigned multiple times to improve alignment, reduce vibration, and increase sensing coverage. Stacked integrated mounting slots were eventually added directly into the chassis geometry, reducing screw count while enabling a third ultrasonic sensor to be added without increasing the robot footprint.

Structural and Layout Improvements

Later revisions widened and reinforced portions of the chassis to improve drivetrain stability and reduce motor shifting during movement. Internal layout changes also improved wire routing, reduced interference with moving components, and created a more organized electrical system during repeated testing.

PCB and Electronics Integration

After competition constraints eliminated the use of breadboards, the chassis was redesigned to support a custom PCB layout while maintaining compact packaging. Additional integrated mounting features were developed for the OLED display, electronics, and support hardware.

Final Chassis Refinement

The final iteration introduced a compact crate-style electronics compartment, reinforced sensor mounts, improved component accessibility, and cleaner system integration while maintaining a lightweight and manufacturable design suitable for reliable autonomous operation.

Electronics and System Architecture

  • Wemos D1R1 (ESP8266) microcontroller for autonomous navigation control
  • Three ultrasonic sensors for real-time obstacle detection and wall tracking
  • DC drivetrain motors with motor driver integration for autonomous movement
  • OLED display for live coordinate and navigation feedback
  • Custom PCB for organized power and signal distribution

The system architecture was designed to balance compact packaging, electrical reliability, and maintainability within the limited chassis footprint. Component placement was carefully arranged to minimize wire interference with moving drivetrain components while still allowing accessible routing for debugging and future modifications.

As the project evolved, the electrical system transitioned from an initial breadboard-based prototype to a more robust PCB-integrated layout to improve reliability and meet final design constraints. Wiring organization and power distribution were refined throughout development using color-coded wiring, integrated routing paths, and secured connections to reduce electrical clutter and improve system consistency during autonomous operation.

Autonomous Navigation Logic

The robot’s autonomous navigation system combined coordinate-based pathfinding with real-time obstacle avoidance to navigate the arena without human input. Using live position data received through the MQTT network and LiDAR tracking system, the robot continuously calculated its distance and orientation relative to target coordinates while adjusting movement toward the next destination point.

Three ultrasonic sensors were used simultaneously for obstacle detection and wall tracking. One sensor faced forward while two side sensors were mounted at 45-degree angles to expand the robot’s field of view and eliminate blind spots near the front corners of the chassis. Sensor readings were constantly monitored by the control algorithm to detect nearby walls and obstacles and trigger directional corrections when necessary.

A major challenge during development was balancing the interaction between the pathfinding system and the obstacle avoidance logic. Early versions of the algorithm occasionally allowed target-navigation calculations to override obstacle detection behavior, causing inconsistent movement near walls and corners. Through repeated testing and tuning, movement thresholds, motor values, and sensor distance conditions were refined to prioritize collision avoidance while still maintaining efficient navigation toward targets.

Additional software refinement focused on compensating for inconsistencies in the LiDAR tracking system and handling occasional arena blind spots where coordinate updates were temporarily lost. The final navigation algorithm successfully guided the robot to multiple target locations while remaining collision-free throughout the final competition run.

Challenges and Engineering Decisions

Sensor Stability and Alignment

Early ultrasonic sensor mounts lacked sufficient retention and would occasionally loosen or fall out during testing, leading to inconsistent obstacle readings. Multiple mounting revisions were developed, including reinforced support geometry, tighter tolerances, and integrated triangular supports to improve sensor stability and maintain reliable alignment during operation.

Wire Management Around Moving Components

Initial wiring layouts allowed cables to interfere with the drivetrain belts, creating inconsistent movement and causing the robot to drift during navigation. Additional structural barriers and revised wire routing paths were incorporated into the chassis to isolate electronics from moving components and improve long-term reliability.

Motor Movement and Chassis Stability

During repeated arena testing, motor shifting and chassis flex introduced inconsistent driving behavior and reduced navigation repeatability. The chassis geometry was widened and reinforced to better support the motors, improve weight distribution, and minimize unwanted movement during acceleration and turning.

Balancing Pathfinding and Obstacle Avoidance

One of the largest software challenges was preventing the coordinate-based pathfinding system from conflicting with the obstacle avoidance logic. Early algorithms occasionally prioritized target movement over nearby obstacle detection, causing unreliable behavior near walls and corners. Extensive tuning of sensor thresholds, motor values, and directional correction logic was required to create stable autonomous navigation.

Transition From Breadboard to PCB Integration

Midway through development, competition constraints required the removal of breadboards from the final design. This forced a redesign of the electronics layout and internal packaging strategy. A compact crate-style compartment and custom PCB integration were developed to maintain organized wiring, accessible components, and a compact overall footprint.

LiDAR Tracking Inconsistencies

The navigation system occasionally encountered arena blind spots where LiDAR position data became unreliable or temporarily unavailable. Additional testing and algorithm refinement were required to improve navigation consistency and reduce the impact of intermittent coordinate loss during autonomous operation.

Final Engineering Takeaways

This project provided extensive experience in mechanical design iteration, embedded system integration, autonomous robotics, and real-world engineering problem solving under strict design constraints. Developing a fully autonomous system required balancing manufacturability, structural stability, sensor performance, electrical organization, software reliability, and overall system packaging within a compact platform.

The project also reinforced the importance of iterative testing and cross-disciplinary integration between mechanical, electrical, and software systems. Small physical changes to sensor placement, wiring layout, chassis geometry, and motor stability often had direct impacts on navigation consistency and autonomous performance, requiring continuous refinement throughout development.

Beyond the technical work, the project strengthened collaborative engineering skills through rapid prototyping, debugging, time management, and coordinating design decisions under tight development deadlines.
